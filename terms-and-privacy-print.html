1. What This Policy Covers

This policy explains what information we collect through Black Hat Community's cards and digital contact pages, how it's used, and who else might see it. It applies separately to cardholders (people who own a Card) and visitors (people who tap or scan a Card and choose to share their own information back).

When you order a Card, you provide us the information you want displayed on your own digital page — which may include your name, phone number, email address, mailing address, job title, business name, photos, social media handles, payment handles, and any other details you choose to include. This information is displayed publicly on your card's page to anyone who taps or visits it, unless you've asked us to omit a specific field.

Important — third-party visitor information: Some cards include a feature that lets someone visiting your page share their own contact information back to you. If you use this feature, you are collecting that visitor's personal information yourself. We provide the tool that makes this possible, but you, as the cardholder, are the one collecting and receiving that information, and you're responsible for using it appropriately.

3. How We Use Cardholder Information

  • To build, host, and maintain your Card's digital page
  • To contact you about your order, your Card, or requested updates

We don't sell your information to third parties, and we don't use it for advertising.

4. Where Information Is Stored

Cardholder information is stored as data files in a private code repository and published to a public website through GitHub and Vercel. We don't collect or store credit card numbers or bank account details. Payments are made directly through third-party apps according to their own privacy practices, which we don't control. We may retain a record that a payment was made and its amount, for our own bookkeeping.

6. Cookies & Analytics

Our site may use basic, privacy-respecting analytics to understand how many people are visiting cards, without tracking individuals across other websites.

  • If you're a visitor who shared your information with a cardholder and want it removed, that request should go to the cardholder directly, since they're the one who received and holds it.
